## Denomination range and the stock problem

One marketplace's US dollar listings ran 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 60, 65, 70, 75, 99, 100, 150 and 200, with separate North America versions at 10 and 35. At the time of checking, the 40, 45, 60 and 75 cards were marked sold out.

That pattern matters more than any single price. Nintendo states plainly that card selection and inventory vary by retailer and region, so there is no universal list of what exists. A size you saw last month may be gone, and the size that matches a $60 game exactly is one of the ones most likely to be out of stock. Combining two smaller cards is normal practice and costs nothing extra.

## Region lock: match the card to your Nintendo Account country

Your Nintendo Account's country decides which card you can redeem. That single rule resolves most of the confusion around cheap codes from overseas sellers.

Nintendo's support documentation describes the payment side from the opposite direction: if the eShop exists for your region, credit cards issued there will not work in a different region's eShop. A card issued for the EU only works in the European eShop. Resellers restate the same restriction in their own terms, warning that a card belonging to one country cannot be used outside it, and they split their catalogues accordingly: North America, Europe, United Kingdom and Poland at one marketplace; North America, Europe and Japan at another.

Practical rules to buy by:

- Buy the card that matches the country set on your Nintendo Account, not the country you happen to be standing in.
- If you have accounts in two regions, keep two separate cards. Wallet balance does not travel.
- A discount on a region you don't have an account for is worth nothing. Check the region label before you check the price.
- The regional credit card restriction is the main reason a prepaid card makes sense at all: if your local card is rejected by the eShop you're shopping in, a card for that region's store is the workaround.

## Redeeming a code: six steps, plus the keyboard that seems broken

Nintendo's step-by-step support page describes the flow as it appears on the console. You need a Nintendo Account, and the Switch 2 or Switch console needs to be online.

1. Find the game in Nintendo eShop on your console and open its product information page. DLC and in-game items appear there too.
2. Select **Proceed to Purchase**.
3. When the console asks how you want to add funds, select **Nintendo eShop Card**.
4. Enter the 16-character prepaid code and select **OK**.
5. Select **Confirm**, then **Purchase**.
6. The download starts automatically. Download status shows on the HOME Menu.

The step that trips people up is step four. The on-screen keyboard disables every character a prepaid code never uses, including the letters O, I and Z, plus commas and hyphens. If a key refuses to respond, the keyboard is behaving as designed, not failing.

Two things that reduce what you pay at that screen:

- **Required Amount Only.** If your wallet is short, choose this instead of a fixed top-up amount. Your existing balance is applied to the price automatically and only the difference goes on your card or PayPal account.
- **My Nintendo Gold Points.** You can apply points before completing the purchase. Their cash value depends on the currency the points were earned in, so the rate is not identical across accounts.

A few payment details from the same page: PayPal funding is limited to Nintendo Account holders in the EU (excluding South Africa) aged 18 and over, and transaction details are emailed to the address registered on your Nintendo Account.

## Expiry, refunds, and the limits people discover too late

**The redemption window.** One reseller lists eShop vouchers as valid for 365 days, meaning the code has to be loaded into a wallet within a year of purchase. That figure comes from a reseller listing, not from Nintendo's gift card page, so treat it as the seller's term and check the specific listing you buy from.

**Refunds.** There is no verified Nintendo refund policy for prepaid cards in the material reviewed here, and policies differ by seller. Unused codes are generally handled under the seller's terms, which is exactly the thing to read before paying at an unfamiliar marketplace, not after.

**What a card cannot buy.** eShop credit pays for digital content, not console hardware. A card will not get you a Switch 2.

**3DS and Wii U are closed.** Since 27 March 2023 you cannot buy games or downloadable content for Wii U or Nintendo 3DS on the eShop. Older guides still describe eShop cards as usable across Switch, 3DS and Wii U; that advice is out of date. Current cards are for the Switch family and Switch 2.

**Switch 2 is supported.** Nintendo's gift card page lists both Nintendo Switch and Nintendo Switch 2 as destinations for the games you buy, and the redemption steps cover both consoles.

**CHOOSE ONE cards are store-only.** Nintendo sells a card type that lets the recipient pick one of three listed Switch games. It is available in-store only at local retailers, so you cannot buy it online no matter which site you land on.

**Membership cards renew themselves.** Nintendo sells 12-month Nintendo Switch Online and Nintendo Switch Online + Expansion Pack cards separately from ordinary eShop cards. The terms state that membership auto-renews after the initial term at the then-current price unless canceled, that it is not available in all countries, and that internet access and a Nintendo Account are required.

## FAQ

**Do I need a card worth exactly the game's price?**
No. Buy the nearest size below it and cover the rest with a card or PayPal using Required Amount Only, so you aren't left with idle balance. The exception is gifting, where a little headroom is usually welcome.

**Can I redeem a US code on a UK account?**
No. The card's region has to match the country on the Nintendo Account you're redeeming into.

**Do these cards expire?**
Reseller listings state a 365-day redemption period. Nintendo's official gift card page does not publish a matching figure, so confirm on the listing before buying.

**Will a card work on Switch 2?**
Yes. Nintendo lists Switch and Switch 2 together for eShop content, and the redemption walkthrough covers both.

**Can I pay for Switch Online with an eShop card?**
Nintendo's direct route is a dedicated 12-month membership card, either standard or with the Expansion Pack. Those are sold through retailers alongside ordinary eShop cards.

**Why won't the keyboard type my code?**
Because O, I, Z, commas and hyphens are not used in prepaid codes and are disabled on purpose.

One full-price game: take the $60 or $65 card if it's in stock, otherwise $50 plus a small top-up at checkout. DLC or a single indie: $10 or $20. A birthday gift: $50, which leaves room for a smaller title or some DLC. A family buying across several accounts: two $50 cards beat one $100 card, since balance can't be split or transferred after redemption.

Two checks before you pay: the region on the card matches the country on your Nintendo Account, and you know the seller's position on unused codes. Everything else, including the exact discount, changes with stock.
